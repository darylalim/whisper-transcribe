# Theme: rejected candidates

Palettes tried before the macOS-native theme in `.streamlit/config.toml`, and what ruled each out — a contrast figure for four of them; for indigo / zinc, that it re-adds what `7daa4f2` removed. The contest had five entrants: `cupertino`, which is the theme that shipped, and the four below plus the indigo / zinc that was never a fresh entrant. Read this before proposing a different palette, so none is re-derived; the theme's live rules and the contrast pairs it must clear are under **Theme** in `CLAUDE.md`.

**Rejected alternatives, so they are not re-derived.** A two-line `stock-blue` (Streamlit's own `#1c83e1` / `#0068c9` as primary and nothing else) fixes the collision at the smallest pin surface and won the maintenance lens, but keeps Streamlit's navy-tinted greys, no field edges and stock light mode's 3.3:1 error red. A DAW-inspired near-black stage with 3:1 borders (`meterbridge`) is the only way to get the white label to 4.5, at the price of a desaturated teal that collapses to grey under protanopia and borders that dominate the layout. A VS Code palette put the dropzone's **Upload** button at 1.02:1 against its well. Re-adding the dropped indigo / zinc (the bundled `minimal` template) would re-add exactly what `7daa4f2` removed. Every candidate was rendered through the real app in both modes and in both the result and the error state before choosing; the harness is the screenshot harness in `docs/screenshots.md`

**`signal-booth` is the second of the two 3:1-rim candidates, and went unrecorded until 2026-09-20.** The **Theme** bullet in `CLAUDE.md` always said *two* candidates pinned 3:1 borders and rendered as wireframes, and named only `meterbridge`; this entry was recovered from the theme session's transcript (`df3652e3`, 2026-09-16) because the render harness was not kept. A broadcast-monitor palette: dark primary `#2d78e9` (`#3b82ee` in the sidebar), page `#0b0e13`, well `#171c25`, text `#e4e7ec`, border `#58657a`, red `#f0565a`. It **won the legibility lens** — one of three judges ranked it first, and its white button label cleared **4.22:1** against the shipped theme's 3.65 — and lost on two measured counts. With a 3:1 `borderColor` (`meterbridge` and `signal-booth` alike) a disabled primary button and a live secondary button carry an identical 3:1 outline and differ only in label alpha, so the field edges stop meaning anything: that is what "rendered as wireframes" measures. And Chrome's fixed `#373737` audio player, which no theme token recolours, sits **1.62:1** above its `#171c25` well — the brightest neutral in the input column, the same failure `meterbridge` has at 1.71 — where the shipped well was chosen 1.17:1 under it on purpose. The judges' leaderboard was `cupertino` 6.33, `meterbridge` 6, `signal-booth` 5.33, `vscode-fluent` 5, `stock-blue` 5.33 with one disqualification.
