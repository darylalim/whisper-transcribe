# Tooling history

The history behind the gate and the toolchain: the gate's timings and the test-count growth that drove them, the one recorded instance of the bundled Streamlit skill's guidance drifting with an upgrade, the inventory of `ty` rules that arrived between 0.0.69 and 0.0.80, how the `torch` install size behind CI's `enable-cache` was measured, and the standalone release workflow that `needs: check` replaced. Moved out of `CLAUDE.md` so it is read on demand rather than loaded into every session. The live rules stay in `CLAUDE.md`: the floor check for any API the bundled skill recommends is under **Commands**, and the hooks, the `Stop` gate and its step order, `ty check`'s warning-as-error behaviour, CI's runner and cache, and the release job's guards are under **Automation**. The pointers here sit in four places under **Automation** — the `ruff -q` / `ty check` bullet, the **CI** bullet, the **`needs: check`** bullet and the `uv lock --check` paragraph — and in the `docs/*.md` bullet under **Architecture**, which is the only pointer for the skill-drift entry, since the **Commands** paragraph it was cut from carries none. Read this before re-measuring the gate or restating the suite's size or timing, before reading a `ty` upgrade's new rules against this repo, before re-deriving why the CI cache is load-bearing, and before touching the release job's `needs:`.

## Commands: the bundled skill's drift

The **Commands** paragraph that begins ``When changing the Streamlit UI (tabs, widgets, theme, layout, caching, fragments), invoke the `developing-with-streamlit` skill`` used to carry one earlier instance of the drift it warns about, between the sentence on `discover.py` resolving the references from the project's `.venv` and the list of 1.63.0-only APIs it still names. The sentence read ``which means every upgrade silently swaps the guidance. It once recommended `st.skeleton` against the old `>=1.58` floor; the 1.63.0 references recommend, without any version qualifier, …`` and now reads ``which means every upgrade silently swaps the guidance. The 1.63.0 references recommend, without any version qualifier, …`` — the cut is the one clause ``It once recommended `st.skeleton` against the old `>=1.58` floor; the``, and the APIs the paragraph goes on to list are unchanged — `st.title/header/subheader(icon=)`, `@st.fragment(key=)` + `st.rerun("<key>")`, `st.status`/`st.expander(type="step")`, `runner.cacheBackgroundRefreshTTLMultiplier`, `wrap=`, `refresh_mode="background"` and `from streamlit.typing import UploadedFile`, none of which exist at the floor. The `st.skeleton` instance is the same drift one release earlier: `st.skeleton` is a 1.59.0 API, absent in 1.58.0, and the bundled `performance.md` / `best-practices.md` recommended it without qualification while the floor sat at `>=1.58`, and adding it as the remote tabs' loading placeholder is what moved the floor to `>=1.59` (the **Lazy Container Execution** section and the `streamlit` bullet under **Dependencies** in `CLAUDE.md` keep that story; the bisect itself is in `docs/dependency-history.md`). The one-command floor check under **Commands** is the standing answer to both instances.

## Automation: the `ty` rule inventory (0.0.69 → 0.0.80)

From the **Automation** bullet that begins ``The two `ruff` steps in the `Stop` hook take `-q`; `ty check` deliberately does **not**``, which keeps the rule that `ty check` exits 1 on any `warn`-level diagnostic and names only `redundant-condition` now. The full inventory, as it stood in that bullet: Of the ten rules added between ty 0.0.69 and 0.0.80, three are on by default: `invalid-module-getattr-call` (error), `missing-slot` (error) and `redundant-condition` (warn); the last is the only one with a realistic surface here — an `if` whose operand is statically always truthy, e.g. a forgotten `()` on a function name. "Here" is this repository's two `.py` files, `streamlit_app.py` and `tests/test_app.py`. The other seven of the ten are off by default and were not listed; if a later `ty` release switches one on by default and it fires on this code, it blocks the gate exactly like an error, since the `Stop` hook passes neither `--exit-zero-on-warning` nor `--error-on-warning`.

## Automation: how the `torch` size was measured

From the **CI** bullet under **Automation** (``**CI** (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`): runs `ruff check` + `ruff format --check` + `ty check` + `pytest` on push to `main` and on PRs``), which keeps the ~520 MB figure and the reason `enable-cache: true` on `setup-uv` is load-bearing, and points here for the measurement. In full: `mlx-whisper` depends on **`torch`**, ~520 MB installed at 2.14.0 (apparent size of `site-packages/torch` with `__pycache__` excluded; `du -sh` reads 542M once bytecode is compiled; ~500 MB at 2.13.0), so a cold cache dominates the run. So the ~520 MB is the apparent size of the package as installed, with no `__pycache__` counted; `du -sh` reads more (542M) because it counts the bytecode once it has been compiled, and the ~20 MB step from 2.13.0 to 2.14.0 is the installed package growing between releases, not a change in what the app loads — `mlx_whisper/torch_whisper.py` is `torch`'s sole user and nothing imports that module, so neither the app nor the tests ever load it (the **CI** bullet keeps that half).

## Automation: the pre-merge release workflow (`needs: check`)

From the **Automation** bullet that begins ``**`needs: check` is the point of the merge.**``, which now keeps only the rule; the **Release** bullet beside it records that there is no separate release workflow because `draft-release.yml` was merged into the `release` job inside `ci.yml`. The defect the merge closed, in full: **`needs: check` is the point of the merge.** As a standalone workflow the release ran independently of CI, so a version bump drafted a release even when `ruff`/`ty`/`pytest` were red. Nothing may publish ahead of a green `check`. The other properties of the merged job — the `contents: write` scope set per job so it never reaches `check`, the `cancel-in-progress` expression that spares a `main` run mid-publish, and the draft-first creation that withholds the tag until publish — are live rules and stay in the **Release** bullets of `CLAUDE.md`.

## Automation: gate timing and test-count history

From the standalone **Automation** paragraph that begins ``**`uv lock --check` must run *first*, ahead of every `uv run` step — the ordering is load-bearing, not cosmetic.**``, which keeps the step's ~0.02 s cost, the five-step gate's ~4.3 s (`pytest` ~4.0 s, `ty` ~0.2 s, the three `uv`/`ruff` steps under 0.1 s combined) and the current count, and points here for how both got there. The history, in full: That is up from the 3.17–3.25 s first recorded at `51d935a`, because the suite grew from 55 to 101 test functions (181 cases) before the YouTube and URL tabs were removed — it was 89 functions (163 cases) after that, and is 100 functions (174 cases) since the sidebar + two-column layout landed, when the gate re-measured at 4.4 s with `pytest` ~3.9 s of it, and 105 functions (181 cases) with the theme cases, `pytest` ~3.8 s — not because of any dependency change.

The same figures in sequence, so the next re-measure can append a line rather than rewrite the paragraph (the commits and dates are from `git log`; the counts and timings are those of the paragraph above):

- `51d935a` (2026-08-10, `chore(hooks): scope the formatter to the repo, close the lockfile gap`) — the five-step gate first recorded at 3.17–3.25 s, with a suite of 55 test functions
- `84e7b40` (2026-09-14) — the ~4.3 s gate / `pytest` ~4.0 s figure the paragraph still quotes entered `CLAUDE.md`, at what that commit's own text called 100 test functions (180 cases); the suite then reached 101 functions (181 cases)
- `9f2e57d` (2026-09-15, `refactor: remove the YouTube and URL input tabs`) — 89 functions (163 cases) after the removal
- the sidebar + two-column layout — 100 functions (174 cases); the gate re-measured at 4.4 s, `pytest` ~3.9 s of it
- the theme cases — 105 functions (181 cases), `pytest` ~3.8 s; the count `CLAUDE.md` carries now

The growth is test count throughout — no dependency change moved the gate — and `pytest` is the whole of it: `ty` is ~0.2 s and the three `uv`/`ruff` steps under 0.1 s combined, which is why the hooks bullet under **Automation** records that adding the two `ruff` steps to the gate cost ~50 ms, indistinguishable within run-to-run noise.
