# Design history

Removed-feature and first-draft history behind five sections of `CLAUDE.md` — **Input Modes**, **Lazy Container Execution**, **Accepted formats**, **Theme** and **Error Handling**: what the YouTube and URL tabs were built from and what their removal changed, the first drafts and earlier claims that a review or a measurement refuted, and the levers and alternatives that were weighed and rejected. Moved out of `CLAUDE.md` so it is read on demand rather than loaded into every session. The live rules — what the code does now, the measurements the tests pin, and the traps that change what to do — stay in `CLAUDE.md` under those five sections, and every entry here opens by naming the bullet its text was cut from, quoting a phrase of that bullet's opener, so the two files cross-reference each other; the pointers back to this file sit in most of those bullets, in the `docs/*.md` bullet under **Architecture**, and in the two rejected-alternatives paragraphs of **Accepted formats** — but not in all of them: the `st.audio` `format=` / `MEDIA_MIME_TYPES` bullet, the caption-wording bullet, the `Filenames are untrusted input` and `What it renders is one muted line` sub-bullets, and the `_error` icon sub-bullet were condensed in place without naming this file, so for their entries this file is the only record. Read it before re-deriving why a decision under those sections was made, before restoring a removed format or reintroducing a remote source, and before restating what an earlier draft or an earlier version of `CLAUDE.md` claimed. Nothing was deleted in the move: each span is reproduced verbatim, in `CLAUDE.md` order, with at most a lead-in sentence and, for a span that was a parenthetical, its outer parentheses dropped. The button-edge pairing, the deleted label-left/control-right grid and the controls card are in `docs/layout-history.md`; the rejected palettes in `docs/theme-candidates.md`; the per-mutation failure maps in `docs/testing-notes.md`.

## Input Modes

### The YouTube and URL tabs

**What they were built from, and what left `uv.lock` with them.** From the **Upload** / **Record** tabs bullet (`each label prefixed with a Material Symbol icon — upload, mic`), whose pointer now says only that the tabs were removed in one piece along with `yt-dlp`. **There used to be YouTube and URL tabs as well** — `yt-dlp` and `urllib` fetches behind an `https?://` gate, `@st.cache_resource` payload caches, a 500 MB `MAX_DOWNLOAD_BYTES` cap, `st.skeleton` loading slots, and a `_RemoteAudio` adapter — removed in one piece along with the `yt-dlp[default,deno]` dependency (nine packages left `uv.lock`, including an 81 MB Deno binary). Several decisions below were shaped by them and are annotated where the reason has changed rather than silently rewritten — "below" being the remaining bullets of **Input Modes** in `CLAUDE.md`, where that last sentence still stands; the entries that follow here, and the ones under **Lazy Container Execution** and **Error Handling**, are the history behind those annotations.

### `st.audio`'s `format=` and `MEDIA_MIME_TYPES`

**The map's coverage, and the `.mpeg4` alias.** From the **`The Upload tab's st.audio call passes an explicit format=`** bullet, on the alias `normalize_upload_file_type` (`TYPE_PAIRS` in `streamlit/elements/lib/file_uploader_utils.py`) silently pairs in beside `.mp4`, which is why `MEDIA_MIME_TYPES` carries nine entries for the eight formats the app declares. A first draft of this bullet said the map mirrored the eight and the fallback was unreachable; both were false for exactly that alias, which the review caught by calling `normalize_upload_file_type` rather than reading the tuples. That review is the origin of the live instruction to verify the list by calling `normalize_upload_file_type` rather than by reading the format tuples.

**The entries the remote paths left behind.** Same bullet; the map is now exactly the uploader's normalized accept list, and `test_media_mime_covers_every_upload_format` pins the equality. The map used to carry `oga`/`ogg`/`flac`/`aac` for the remote paths, which never consulted the format tuples; the uploader rejects them server-side, so those entries were unreachable once the tabs went.

**The dotfile case.** Same bullet, which keeps the rule (`rpartition(".")`, matching the uploader's own `endswith`) and the test; this is the draft that got it wrong. `_media_mime` reads the extension with `rpartition(".")` rather than `Path.suffix`, to match the uploader's own `endswith` rule: a file literally named `.mp3` is accepted as an MP3, and `Path.suffix` would call it extensionless and hand it the fallback — which a first draft of this change did, and then pinned that wrong output in `test_media_mime[dotfile]` until the review caught it (the case now expects `audio/mpeg` and fails on `Path.suffix`, measured).

### The caption's wording

**Why "transcribed locally" rather than "nothing leaves your Mac".** From the **`Under the title sits a one-line st.caption`** bullet ("Audio and video, transcribed and translated locally on your Mac."), which says *transcribed* locally because the first transcription downloads the model weights, so the flat claim would be false for exactly the first-time visitor the line exists for. It used to be false in two of four input modes as well, while the YouTube and URL tabs existed; the wording survives the removal because the weights download still does.

### The Transcribe dispatch

**Where the mis-dispatch bug was first found.** From the **`The Transcribe button dispatches from the tab that is open`** bullet, whose flat `or`-chain (`uploaded_files or [recorded_audio]`) let a sticky upload outrank the recording whose player was on screen. The bug was first found with the since-removed YouTube/URL tabs, whose sources existed only while their tab was open, so an earlier upload outranked a URL whose preview had just rendered. The two-sticky-source form above is the same bug with less to hide behind. "Above" is that bullet's Upload + Record case in `CLAUDE.md`: both tabs declare their widgets in ungated tab bodies, so both values are sticky across tab switches, and only the results `st.subheader` — after a full model pass — says which one ran.

### The `seek(0)` rewind

**Why the `isinstance` guard went.** From the **`It also seek(0)s the file before reading it`** sub-bullet of `_handle_transcription`: `_handle_transcription` also `seek(0)`s the file before reading it (unconditionally — the `isinstance(uploaded_file, UploadedFile)` guard it used to carry existed only because the remote tabs' `_RemoteAudio` adapter had no cursor). With `_RemoteAudio` gone every source is an `UploadedFile`, which subclasses `io.BytesIO`, and the guard had nothing left to exclude.

**The refuted rerun mechanism.** Same sub-bullet, whose live text keeps the conclusion — `register_widget` hands the script `deepcopy(widget_value)` on every run, so no rerun path delivers an already-read object — and sends the mechanism it replaced here. **The rewind covers a same-object double read within one run, and an earlier version of this line claimed more than that.** It said the deserialized widget value survives every rerun as the same object, so a second **Transcribe** on an unchanged recording would hand `_transcribe` `b""` once the Record tab stopped rendering the `st.audio` preview whose `_marshall_av_media` had been rewinding it. That mechanism is wrong at 1.61.1 and 1.63.0 alike: `WStates.__getitem__` does cache `Value(deserialized)`, but `register_widget` hands the script `deepcopy(widget_value)` (`session_state.py`, "so that reference types can't be accidentally mutated by user code"), so the cached original is never read and every run — browser-initiated or `st.rerun()` — receives a fresh copy at cursor 0. The `seek(0)` stays for the one real hazard, a same-object double read within one run, which is what `test_handle_transcription_rewinds_the_cursor_before_reading` constructs.

### Filenames and `_escape_markdown`

**The same-origin claim.** From the **`Filenames are untrusted input`** sub-bullet: an unescaped `![](…)` in a filename would fire an image request on the *happy* path, to whatever destination the name spells — and a filename needs no `/` to point off-site, since `https:host` is a valid destination that resolves to `https://host/` (an earlier version of this line said the request could only be same-origin; `test_error_escapes_the_whole_message[scheme_only_destination]` pins the shape).

**How the two gaps hid behind the "parse-time only" framing.** From the **`Two parse-time constructs that once hid behind that "parse-time only" framing, both now closed`** bullet — the framing is the preceding `_escape_markdown` bullet's rule that the helper covers *inline* parse-time constructs only and three of Streamlit's extensions run outside the parse — which now carries the two constructs without the history of how they were missed. **Two gaps that used to hide behind that "parse-time only" framing, both now closed.** Stating the limitation as a *rule* invited checking the list rather than the boundary, and both of these are parse-time constructs the rule implied were already covered: the `&` of an HTML character reference (`characterReference` is parse-time, and the escape class omitted it, so `clip&#58;streamlit&#58;.mp3` decoded into a live `:streamlit:` at every sink) and the block markers `#`, `>`, `-`, `+`, `|` and ordered-list `N.` / `N)` (every block construct needs a *line start*, which a newline in a filename supplied). Each has its own sub-bullet under that heading in `CLAUDE.md`, with the fix — `&` in the class, and the whitespace collapse inside `_escape_markdown` for the block markers.

**The earlier wrong claim about `st.subheader`.** From the **`st.subheader`** sub-bullet under those two, which is the second of the two sinks that render without the frontend's `isLabel` flag; "this file" in what follows is `CLAUDE.md`. This one is genuinely counter-intuitive and an earlier version of this file got it backwards, asserting the heading was "hardened for free". It is not. The heading component splits the body at its first newline and renders everything after it through a bare `StreamlitMarkdown` with no `isLabel` and no `disallowedElements`, exactly like an alert body, which the live sub-bullet spells out with the bundle code at 1.61.1 and 1.63.0.

**When a newline in a filename was reachable at both sinks.** From the paragraph under that sub-bullet that opens `It *was* reachable at both` — "both" being `st.error`'s body and `st.subheader`'s remainder — whose pointer now says only that the removed URL tab percent-decoded filenames off the URL path. It *was* reachable at both while the URL tab existed — `_fetch_url_audio` percent-decoded the filename off the URL path, so `%0A` arrived as a real newline and a URL ending `/clip%0A%0A%23%20Big%0A%0A%3E%20quote.mp3` rendered an `<h1>` and a blockquote inside the error box **and** inside the results card. No browser path delivers a newline, as the live paragraph goes on to say, but a direct PUT to the upload route still can, which is why both tests stay.

### The empty results hint

**The "mirrors the dropzone" comment.** From the **`What it renders is one muted line in a 54 px bordered box at the top of the results column`** sub-bullet of `_display_transcription`, which keeps the geometry — level with the tab strip at y=223, in exactly the slot the `st.status` box takes once a run starts (y=223..263) — and the rule that follows. A first draft of the source comment said it "mirrors the dropzone, the same height"; the dropzone is 68 px and starts at y=279, below the tab strip, so it does neither — and pushing the box down to the dropzone with an `st.space`, which the live bullet forbids, would hard-code the tab strip's height and give up the positional swap with the `st.status` box.

**The three drafts of the gate, in order.** From the **`The gate is elif not batch_just_ran, and it took three drafts`** sub-bullet, which keeps the two constraints and the final form: `batch_just_ran = bool(transcribe_clicked and audio_sources and not time_range_error)` at the call site, the fragment registered only when `session_state["transcription"]` is non-empty, and the hint rendered `elif not batch_just_ran`. `_handle_transcription` publishes `[]` before its loop, so a batch in which every file failed leaves an empty list behind, and a first draft keyed the hint on `not transcriptions` alone, which rendered it directly under that batch's failure alert — a "nothing has happened here" line beneath evidence that something had. A second draft keyed it on the *key's absence*, which fixed the click run and broke every run after it: the status and the alerts exist only on the click run, nothing ever deletes the key, so from the next rerun on — a setting toggled to retry, a tab switch — the column was blank for the rest of the session, for exactly the user the hint exists for. A third threaded a `batch_just_ran` flag into `_display_transcription` as a fragment argument, which worked but needed a paragraph on why `st.fragment` replaying a stale argument on fragment-only reruns was harmless. The call-site form is what made `_display_transcription` a zero-argument results renderer again, with no fragment registered when there is nothing to rerun.

## Lazy Container Execution

**The gated-fetch pattern the remote tabs used.** From the paragraph opening **`Nothing in the tab bodies is gated any more, but the tabs still carry st.tabs(..., on_change="rerun", key="input_tabs")`** — "this section" in what follows is **Lazy Container Execution** in `CLAUDE.md`. The gating that this section used to describe — the YouTube and URL tabs guarding their **fetch** on `tab.open` while always declaring the `st.text_input` that fed it, the fetches running below every control and writing back into an `st.container()` slot reserved inside the tab, an `st.skeleton` sized to the player filling that slot while the download ran — went with those tabs. Two lessons from it are worth keeping even with no current caller: gate the side effect, never the widget (Streamlit removes state for widgets absent from a run, so a whole `with tab:` body behind `if tab.open:` silently clears a typed value on every tab switch), and `st.skeleton`'s context-manager form does not redirect bare `st.*` calls into itself (enter the target slot first, `with slot, st.skeleton(...)`). Both stay in full as the two bullets under that paragraph in `CLAUDE.md`, along with the `st.skeleton` floor note that sent the floor to `>=1.59`.

**The third accepted consequence.** From the paragraph opening **`Two accepted consequences`** — a server rerun per tab switch, and that a tab switch can interrupt an in-flight transcription, which is why `_handle_transcription` publishes results progressively — whose closing parenthetical used to open with a third. (A third consequence — that a remote source could not *enable* **Transcribe** from another tab, because it existed only while its own tab was open — went with the remote tabs.) The rest of that parenthetical, that both remaining sources are sticky so the button's enabled state depends only on whether anything is loaded, still stands in `CLAUDE.md`.

## Accepted formats

**Two other levers, weighed and rejected.** From the paragraph on the `wide` layout's fluid input column, after `which is why the wider gutter was tried and reverted` — the `gap="medium"` experiment that cost each column 8px and the 13-inch MacBook Air's 1470px viewport its fit by 1px. Two other levers were weighed and rejected: dropping `mkv` (325px, fits 1440 but not 1366) narrows the accepted-upload surface as a side effect of a layout change, and a `[3, 2]` split (threshold ~1300px) leaves the input column mostly empty on the display the layout was built for. Both are still named in the live paragraph's pointer; the figures are here. The `st.columns(2, gap="small")` bullet under **Input Modes** points here for the same `[3, 2]` rejection, and used to carry its other cost — the split would also tighten the transcript measure to ~90 cpl, against the ~110 the equal columns give — which that bullet's pointer still states beside the ~1300 px threshold.

**Two rejected alternatives to a short `type` list.** From the standalone paragraph that opened `Two rejected alternatives, both worth not re-deriving`. The `enforce_filename_restriction` bypass stays in the live paragraph's pointer because it is a trap that changes what to do; this is the full text:

Two rejected alternatives, both worth not re-deriving:

- **An `st.caption` restating the full list below the uploader.** Works, but adds a permanent line of chrome under the dropzone to compensate for a truncated label. Rejected on UI grounds
- **Shortening `type` to the MIME shortcuts (`type=["audio", "video"]`).** Renders as just `audio, video` — the frontend formatter strips the `/*` and skips the uppercasing it applies to bare extensions — and always fits. But `enforce_filename_restriction` (`streamlit/elements/lib/file_uploader_utils.py`) **skips server-side validation entirely** when any entry contains a `/`, because the backend can't tell whether a file was meant to match a MIME pattern or an extension. Bare extensions are what keep that bypass guard active

**The nine formats removed when the list was cut to eight.** From the **Accepted formats** paragraph that opened with those words, whose pointer keeps only that reasons were recorded for `aac` and `ogg`. Formats removed when the list was cut to eight, in case one needs restoring: `aac` (near-redundant — standalone `.aac` is rare, AAC audio almost always arrives inside `.m4a`), `flac`, `ogg` (Telegram voice notes, where WhatsApp uses `.opus`), `aiff`, `avi`, `wmv`, `flv`, `mpeg`, `3gpp`. Restoring one means an entry in `MEDIA_MIME_TYPES` as well (`test_media_mime_covers_every_upload_format` pins equality against the uploader's normalized accept list) and a README edit in the same commit, and it lengthens the dropzone hint that `test_format_list_fits_the_dropzone_hint` guards with its 60-character ceiling.

## Theme

**The over-claim on primary-as-text, and the wrong brief on hover.** From the **`The dark primary sits on the text side of an impossible triangle`** bullet under **Theme**, which keeps every contrast figure — the tab label at 4.66 on the page, the selected **Transcript format** option at **3.42:1** on the sidebar's 10%-tinted well (the "sidebar cell" below), the white label at 3.65 — and drops the two corrections that produced them. (An earlier version of this line claimed "every primary-as-text pair ≥4.5"; the code review caught the sidebar cell.) The white button label sits at **3.65:1** — above the 3:1 large-text / component floor, above stock's 3.3, and hover *darkens* the fill (`darken(primary, 15%)` in HSL, both modes, at 1.59.0 and 1.63.0 alike; the brief for this work said "mixed toward the text colour" and was wrong), which lifts white-on-hover to 6.3. `test_theme_contrast` pins the tinted segment and the white label at ≥3.0 and primary-as-text at ≥4.5 only where it sits on the page, which is the accepted side of the triangle.

## Error Handling

**The `_error` call-site count over time.** From the **`The icon`** sub-bullet of `_error`, whose live text keeps the seven-to-two arc but not the intermediate count: There are two call sites left (the per-file failure replay in `_handle_transcription` and the **Time range** validation error) — seven at introduction, six once `_handle_transcription`'s two inline failure branches folded into the single replay-loop call, two now that the four remote-tab sites are gone — and the helper is kept at two for the same reason it was introduced at seven. The four remote-tab sites went with the YouTube and URL tabs, as did `st.info`'s one call site, the URL tab's YouTube-link redirect with its explicit `:material/smart_display:`.

**When library exception text was untrusted.** From the **`The escaping, and this is the part that is not cosmetic`** sub-bullet, whose pointer says only that library exception text can be untrusted too and was while the remote tabs existed. While the remote tabs existed the exception text itself was untrusted too: `yt-dlp`'s `UnsupportedError` is literally `f"Unsupported URL: {url}"`, and `HTTPError.__str__` embeds a server-controlled reason phrase. Escaping the *whole* message rather than the filename span is what made that a non-event, and it is why the whole-message rule stays even though the library text reaching `_error` today is `mlx_whisper`'s and ffmpeg's.

**The pre-sidebar placement of the Time range alert.** From the **`Where the two call sites render is a layout decision, and both halves are pinned`** sub-bullet; "It" in what follows is the **Time range** alert, which now renders in the main input column directly above the Transcribe button while the `st.text_input` that produced it sits in the sidebar's collapsed Advanced options expander. It used to render outside the expander *and* outside the bordered controls card, above the button, while every control lived in the main column — the sidebar move is what turned "outside the card" into "in a different column from the input". The reason it already sat outside both is the one the live sub-bullet keeps — the alert belongs with the button it disables, not with the input, so a disabled **Transcribe** always shows its reason beside the button, even with the expander collapsed. The controls card itself, and the label-left/control-right grid it held, are in `docs/layout-history.md`.
